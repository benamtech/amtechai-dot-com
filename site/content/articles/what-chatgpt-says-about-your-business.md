{{< answer >}}
Ask ChatGPT the four questions below, word for word, in fresh chats: who's the best in your trade and town, who to call in a hurry, what it knows about you by name, and how you compare. Ask the first one three times. Write down who it names, what it says about you, and which pages it links to. Those pages are where the answer comes from, and where you fix it.
{{< /answer >}}

## Question 1: Who's the best in your trade and town?

This is the question that sends customers to someone. Ask it the way a customer types it, three times, each in a new chat.

{{< prompt title="Ask this three times" helper="Use your trade and your town, the way a customer says them." >}}
best [roofer] in [Louisville, KY]
{{< /prompt >}}

Write down every business it names in each answer. Count how often each one comes up. The ones named every time are the ones ChatGPT is most sure of. Note whether you're named once, sometimes, or never.

When we asked this for 165 local businesses in six trades in November 2026, ChatGPT never named 132 of them.

## Question 2: Who should I call right now?

Urgent questions are worded differently, and they're the ones that turn into jobs tonight.

{{< prompt title="Ask it like a customer in a hurry" helper="Pick the real emergency in your trade." >}}
My [AC just died and it's 90 degrees inside] in [East Nashville]. Who can come tonight?
{{< /prompt >}}

See whether it names anyone who works at night, and whether that's you. If your website and profiles don't say you take emergency calls, ChatGPT can't say it either.

## Question 3: What do you know about my business?

Now ask about yourself by name. This is what a customer sees before they call you back.

{{< prompt title="Ask about your business by name" helper="Use your business name exactly as it appears on Google." >}}
What do you know about [Ridgeline Roofing] in [Louisville, KY]? What's their phone number, hours and the services they offer?
{{< /prompt >}}

Check every fact against your Google listing. Look for an old phone number, wrong hours, a service you don't offer, the wrong town, or "I don't have much information about this business". Each one is something you can fix.

## Question 4: How do I compare?

{{< prompt title="Ask how you compare" helper="Use one of the businesses it named in question 1." >}}
How does [Ridgeline Roofing] compare with [the company it named first] for a roof replacement in [Louisville]?
{{< /prompt >}}

The answer tells you what ChatGPT thinks sets the other business apart: more reviews, a list they're on, certifications, a clearer website. That's your to-do list.

## Read the links under the answer

ChatGPT often links the pages it read. For local businesses these are usually "best of" lists (Expertise.com, BestProsInTown, ThreeBestRated, local "top rated" pages), review sites, trade directories like Houzz or Zillow, and the businesses' own websites. Open each one and check whether you're on it. If you're not, that's the first thing to change.

## What to fix first

1. **Get on the lists it read.** Most have a free way to submit. In our checks, businesses on at least one cited list were named 36% of the time, against 14% for businesses on none.
2. **Make your facts match everywhere.** One name, one phone, the same hours, on Google, your website and every profile.
3. **Claim your page in ChatGPT's business directory** and fill in every field. [Here's how](/articles/how-to-claim-your-business-on-chatgpt).
4. **Put your services and towns on your website as plain text,** one service per section, so ChatGPT can read what you do and where.
5. **Keep reviews coming,** and answer each one.
6. **Ask again in a month,** the same four questions, and compare.
