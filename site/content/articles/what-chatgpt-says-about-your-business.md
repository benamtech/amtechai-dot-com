A customer used to ask a neighbour who to call. Now he asks ChatGPT, and ChatGPT answers with three names and a list of pages it read. If your business is not in those three names, you do not exist for that customer, and nothing tells you it happened.

This page is how to see what ChatGPT says about your business, why it says it, and what you can fix yourself.

## The answer is three names and a list of pages

Ask ChatGPT the question a customer asks — "best plumber in Nashville, TN", "best realtor in Tacoma, WA" — and you get a short list of businesses, a sentence or two about each, and links to the pages the answer came from. Those links are the useful part. They are the pages the answer was built from, and most of them are pages you can get onto.

One answer is not a measurement. In our own runs on 15 November 2026, asking the same question in the same town three times gave three different answers: the top names held, the third name changed, and a business named in the first answer was not named in the third. That is why we report "named in 0 of 3 answers on 15 November 2026" rather than a position. It is also why an owner who checks once, sees his name, and stops looking is fooling himself.

## Where the answer comes from

We read the citations in 40 answers about 20 real businesses, in six towns, on 15 November 2026. The sources ChatGPT named, in order of how often: the Better Business Bureau, Zillow, Google, Yelp, BuildZoom, Realtor.com, and — in 15 of the 40 answers — a "best of" list page from a site most owners have never heard of. The business's own website was cited in 38 of the 40 answers, so your site matters, but it is not enough on its own.

That is the whole mechanism, as far as anyone outside OpenAI can see it: you are in the answer if the pages it reads say you are.

Two things follow, and they are the parts most owners get wrong.

**A directory listing is not a formality.** It is the source. If the list ChatGPT reads for your trade and town does not have you, no amount of work on your own site puts you in that answer.

**Disagreeing facts cost you the call.** In the same 20 businesses, ChatGPT gave two different phone numbers for 5 of them, and the Google listing disagreed with the business's own website on the number in 4 of the 17 that published one. Nine of the 20 Google listings said "open 24 hours", which is true for an emergency plumber and a mistake for a realtor — and ChatGPT repeated it or contradicted it. A customer who cannot reach you calls the next name on the list.

## What claiming your business page in ChatGPT does

On 10 November 2026 OpenAI opened business pages inside ChatGPT. A business can claim its page in Business Hub, set its own phone number, hours and services, see how often it is mentioned, and, if it is eligible, add an agent that answers customers' questions. It is free.

It is worth doing, and it is not the thing that gets you recommended. OpenAI says claiming does not make ChatGPT recommend a business. We say the same thing, because the alternative is selling an owner a promise the product does not make. What claiming buys you is that the facts ChatGPT shows are yours, and not a guess assembled from three other websites.

## What to do this week, for free

1. **Find the lists.** Ask ChatGPT your customer's question, three times, and open every link in the answer. The pages that come up again and again are the ones to be on. Most of them have an "add your business" or "claim your listing" form.
2. **Use one phone number everywhere.** Pick the number you answer, and put it in the same form on your website, your Google listing, and every directory. If you use a tracking number, say so on the site, in words.
3. **Make your hours agree.** The same hours in the same words, everywhere, including what happens after them.
4. **Answer every review, and ask every finished job for one.** The review count on the card ChatGPT shows is often not the count on Google, and a customer reading nine reviews against your competitor's six hundred does not call.

None of that costs money. All of it costs an afternoon, once, and a few minutes a week after.
