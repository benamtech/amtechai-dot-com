Ask ChatGPT for the best plumber in your town and you get an answer: a few names, a line about each, and the pages it read. Under that answer there is now a small card with an advertiser's name on it. That card is a ChatGPT ad, and contractors are being sold them already. Here is how they work, what they cost, and what is actually known.

## What a ChatGPT ad is and where it appears

The ad sits below a ChatGPT response, in its own slot, marked as sponsored. Each one carries the advertiser's name, a favicon, a title, a line of copy, a landing page and a square image.

Two things follow. You cannot buy your way to the top of an answer, because there is no rank for sale. And the creative has to read as an ad: OpenAI's ad policies require ads to be clearly distinguishable from the ChatGPT product, so "recommended by ChatGPT" is a policy breach.

Placement decides media plans: ads appear only on the Free and Go plans, and Plus, Pro and Enterprise stay ad-free (OpenAI, October 21, 2026). You are buying the free tier of ChatGPT.

## How the account is opened, and who may run one for you

Signup takes a website URL. Ads Manager will draft a first ad and campaign during setup, or start empty, and your ads go live once they pass review.

Three names get set, and they are not interchangeable. The Brand Name is the public name shown alongside your ads, and changing it triggers brand review and pauses serving. The Legal Name is your registered entity, used for billing and verification. That is why an agency cannot quietly run your campaign under its own name: the brand on the card has to be yours.

For anyone managing several advertisers, OpenAI's instruction is one line: "If you manage multiple advertisers, create a separate account for each advertiser." One login switches between them. You open the account, then invite the manager from Settings, Users, Invite, as Admin or Member, and you keep Admin. Once you belong to 10 or more ad accounts you cannot create another yourself, but you can still be invited into existing ones.

Money works the same way. On a self-serve account billed by card, an agency card does not make the agency the invoice customer, and OpenAI will not reissue invoices in another company's name (billing FAQ). Your card pays OpenAI. A manager bills you a fee for the work.

## What you can target

OpenAI's help center says you can target "states or regions, cities, markets, and postal codes where available". The API is narrower: it documents country, region and market, looked up by ID, with up to 2,500 IDs in an include or exclude list. A market is a metro, like "San Francisco - Oakland - San Jose", not a town and not a zip.

Markets and regions are usable today. Postal codes are promised "where available" and appear nowhere in the API docs, so "only the four zips you service" is a promise to hold until your account's setup search shows them.

The second lever is context hints, a list of topics per ad group describing where your service fits. OpenAI says the hints guide matching but "are not exact-match keywords and do not guarantee delivery in specific conversations". Keyword bidding does not exist here, and no search term report comes back afterwards.

There are no demographic levers either: no age, gender, income or interest targeting exists anywhere in the documentation. Custom audiences need at least 25,000 matched users to include, which one location will never have, so the only sensible use is exclusion. Suppress your customer list.

## What it costs

OpenAI's minimum daily budget is $25 in USD.

Twenty-five dollars a day is about $760 a month. At the $5.80 per click the agency Brafton measured across its own 72-day campaign, that buys about 131 clicks. Published click-to-lead rates then land the total between half a lead and eight leads a month. Brafton's own conversion rate was 0.38%, which is roughly half a lead at 131 clicks. At the $92.21 cost per lead StubGroup reported for its client, it is about eight.

That arithmetic is the argument about the floor. At $25 a day the media buys almost nothing, so a real test starts at $50 to $100 a day.

OpenAI also says plainly that "ChatGPT Ads does not yet have performance benchmarks across advertisers, industries, or campaign types". Every figure above comes from an agency's own campaign.

## The sponsored business agent, and its limits

An ad can carry a Start chat button that opens a business agent, built inside Ads Manager under Business agent, then Agent builder. It requires the Conversion optimization objective and "Enable agent chats", and OpenAI says it is "rolling out to eligible advertisers in Ads Manager over the coming weeks".

Three limits matter. Advertisers do not receive individual chats or transcripts, only patterns, and insights unlock after more than 25 conversations. You cannot bring your own agent or connect your own plugin. Billing does not change, because the campaign's settings apply and there is no separate charge for a conversation.

It is also not the Business Hub agent. Settings do not sync, and access is granted per ad account, so whether your account has it is a question for your setup rather than for a policy page. It is rolling out, not finished.

## What is actually known about results

OpenAI's own customer page is the only home-services result it publishes itself. Angi reported a 38%+ improvement in ROAS quarter over quarter (Q3 against Q2) and 34% lower cost per lead since launching conversion-optimized campaigns. Angi is a home-services marketplace at scale, its numbers are improvements rather than absolute costs, and the gains came after conversion-optimized campaigns, which have to be enabled on an account by OpenAI.

The best-looking contractor case study on the open web is WKND's "109 won jobs" for HVAC, plumbing and roofing, with a $115.74 cost per lead. It is labelled "Modeled results" in its own header. Nobody measured those numbers; a model produced them.

The independent reading is thinner. A working paper covering 102 advertiser sites put revenue at $0.89 per US session against Google's $2.45. Adthena, looking at 30 enterprise brands from July to September, found 52% could not recoup their spend and 26% matched or beat Google.

What nobody has published is a result for a single-location contractor measured by that contractor. OpenAI's story index carries seven stories and one home-services entry, Angi.

## Why real estate agents cannot be sold this

OpenAI's ad policies say it directly: "Ads for individual job listings or housing rentals or sales are prohibited." The changelog dates that clarification to version 1.4, August 2026. Real estate is not among the allowed verticals either, and the policy says all other categories are disallowed at launch.

HUD's guidance from April 29, 2024 tells advertisers to identify housing-related ads to the platform, and tells platforms to avoid targeting that works as a proxy for a protected class. ChatGPT Ads has no housing category, no special ad category, no declared-audience step. There is no way to tell it an ad is about housing, so the first recommendation cannot be met, and HUD puts the liability on the advertiser.

We do not sell ads to real estate agents, and the reason is the missing control, not the copy.

## The risks worth saying out loud

Approval is a gate with no published timeline. Every ad is reviewed before it serves, a rejection shows as Not serving, and landing pages that block OpenAI's crawlers fail review. OpenAI also reserves the right to decline or remove advertisers for any reason, including its own business interests.

The floor is a trap. Half a lead to eight leads a month will not tell you whether the channel works for your trade.

There is no cost-per-lead history for your trade to plan against, and attribution is last-touch and 24 to 48 hours late. Phone leads stay invisible unless call tracking or a CRM fires a lead event server-side, and for most trades the phone is the lead. Reach is the free tier only, so the customer who already pays for ChatGPT never sees your card.
