An AI agent management platform is software that builds, connects, runs and oversees a business's AI agents in one place. Each agent gets one job, the tools and sign-ins that job needs, and rules for what it may do on its own; the owner sees every agent's work, results and costs on one screen.
{{< /answer >}}

## What the platform does

A business that puts AI to work soon has more than one agent: one pricing estimates, one following up quotes, one keeping the books, one working on how the business shows up in Google and ChatGPT. Each needs the same things, and the platform is what gives them:

| The platform | What it means for the business |
|---|---|
| **Builds** the agents | A team is made for the work the business actually has, each agent with its own job and skills |
| **Connects** them | Each agent signs in to the software it needs, such as QuickBooks, Gmail, Procore or Jobber, and works where no connection exists through the screen |
| **Runs** them | Agents work on a schedule or when work arrives, several jobs at once, without someone prompting each step |
| **Oversees** them | One screen shows what each agent is doing, what it finished and what it needs |
| **Governs** them | Standing rules in plain words say what each agent may do on its own; every action leaves a record |
| **Accounts** for them | The cost of every job is shown, so the owner knows what the work cost |

## How it differs from a chatbot or an automation tool

A **chatbot** such as ChatGPT is a tool you drive. It is excellent at a draft or an answer, but it starts from what you type, and the work stops when you stop.

An **automation tool** follows a path someone drew: when a form arrives, add a row and send an email. It is reliable for fixed paths and breaks when the work needs judgement.

An **agent** reads the situation and decides the steps. Given a plan set, it reads the drawings, measures them and prices them from the business's own book. An agent management platform is what lets a business run a team of them safely: the right access, the right rules, and a record of the work.

## What to look for

- **Agents made for your business, not picked from a menu.** The work a roofer, a real estate firm and a restaurant group hand over is different.
- **Work inside the systems you already run.** Agents that need you to copy data into a new tool will not last.
- **Live visibility.** You should see each agent's current job, its finished work and what it is waiting on, across every location or client.
- **Rules in plain words and a record of every action.** That is what lets an owner hand over more work each week.
- **Clear costs.** Each job should show what it cost.
- **No developer required.** If building an agent needs an engineer, it is a framework, not a platform for a business.
