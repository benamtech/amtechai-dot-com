## Query Fan-Out As Editorial Strategy

Google describes query fan-out as concurrent related queries used to retrieve more context for a user question. AMTECH uses the same idea before writing:

- Direct query: the literal question.
- Definitional fan-outs: terms the reader or agent must understand.
- Practical fan-outs: what the reader is trying to do.
- Comparison fan-outs: alternatives and substitutes.
- Risk/compliance fan-outs: what can go wrong.
- Image/video fan-outs: what visual evidence or explanatory asset would help.
- Local/industry fan-outs: where the mechanism changes by place or vertical.

Fan-out is not permission to publish thin variants. It is a map for what belongs inside the article, what becomes adjacent nodes, and what surfaces the evidence ecosystem needs.

## Citation Selection Versus Citation Absorption

The goal is not merely to be cited. The article must contribute a claim or mechanism that an AI answer would actually use.

Treat citation absorption as a separate optimization target from citation selection.

Write for absorption by:

- Making the answer explicit in the first screen.
- Giving a named distinction or mechanism.
- Attaching each factual claim to a source ID.
- Adding concrete tables, checklists, and failure modes.
- Making internal edges legible with relationship reasons.

## Claim Provenance Discipline

Every factual claim has two questions:

source ownership is separate from topical relevance.

1. Does the source support the claim?
2. Is the source the right owner of that claim?

Official docs support platform behavior. Primary reporting supports reported events and figures. Research papers support measured methods and caveats. Operator voice supports language and pain, not universal market truth. Vendor docs support product context, not independent demand proof.

## Agent-Friendly Surface Design

For article briefs, agent-friendly means:

- Semantic headings that reflect the mechanism.
- Tables and checklists with visible labels.
- FAQ only when the article visibly answers those questions.
- Schema that mirrors visible content.
- Markdown/OKF projection as an orientation surface.
- Images with clear subject matter, not vague atmosphere.
- Internal links that describe relationships, not "read more."

## Pinterest And Image Arbitrage

Every major article should create at least three visual briefs:

1. A clean explanatory diagram.
2. A workflow/operator visual.
3. A 2:3 Pinterest pin.

Pinterest pin rules:

- Use the article entity in the pin title and description.
- Make the visual subject inspectable.
- Use headline text only if it can be read cleanly.
- Avoid viral/clickbait framing.
- Treat the pin as a helpful doorway to the article, not a disconnected promotional asset.
