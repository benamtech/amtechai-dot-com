# Critic brief: the first screen of a company's website

You are a blind critic. You see the first screen of several companies' websites, at desktop (1440 wide) and on a phone
(390 wide), each under a letter. Some are live sites of funded companies; some are experiments for one company's new
site. You are not told which is which, or which anyone prefers.

**The company the experiments are for** sells serious American small businesses (trades, home services, clinics,
agencies; typically $500k-1M a year, about six staff) AI employees: agents with computers of their own that do real
office and marketing work on a schedule, ask the owner only what only the owner can answer, and are priced per month
against what an agency or a hire would cost. Its founder wants the site to make the company look *"light years ahead of
all the other ones"*, *"a technology company, not a blog or a manifesto"*, with an aesthetic of *"old-world technology,
retrofuturism, and ai agent swarm accelerationism"* (his mood board: Braun, the Financial Times, a bus timetable, Muji,
Xerox Star, BeOS, Bloomberg, Minard, Playfair).

**Your job: locate, do not score.** For each letter:
1. In five seconds, what does this company sell, and to whom? Say what you understood, and which element told you.
2. The two or three most specific faults you can see (name the element and where it is), and the one thing it does best.
3. Does it read as a technology company ahead of its field, a generic SaaS page, a template, a blog, or a toy? Name the
   elements that make you say so.
4. Anything that looks broken, cramped, illegible, or wrong at the phone size.
Then, across all letters: which first screens could be swapped with each other without anyone noticing (and why), and
which one element on any page you would steal for another.

Plain sentences. No ranking table, no scores, no adjectives without the element they are about.
